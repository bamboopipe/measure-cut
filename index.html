Open pipe: length to cut full

Notes

Pitch mode: enter the current and the target pitch as note names in scientific pitch notation, A4 = 440 Hz: A4, C#5, Bb3, with an optional cents offset (A4+12, Bb3-8). A target pitch without an octave resolves to the nearest matching note to the current one; an exact tie goes upward.

Cents mode: enter how far to raise the pitch; 100 cents is one semitone. A negative value lowers the pitch, so the pipe would have to be lengthened.

End correction: sound does not stop exactly at an open end. The vibrating air extends slightly beyond it, so the pipe behaves as if it were a little longer than its physical length. Each open end adds about 0.61 × the inner radius, so for a pipe open at both ends the effective length is the physical length plus 0.61 × the inner diameter. Pitch follows the effective length, so the cut is calculated on it. The "end correction" line shows how much of the cut comes from it, and "without end correction" is the cut you would get by ignoring it. This assumes both ends are open; a blown end (mouthpiece or embouchure) has its own correction, which is not modelled here.

Diameter is the inner bore diameter; for a tapered bore use the mean of the two ends. Without it the end correction is omitted and the cut comes out slightly too small, more so for wide pipes and larger pitch changes.

The last line, linear L x 5.6126% x s, is the shortcut: the entered length times 5.6126% times the pitch change in decimal semitones (cents / 100). It is shown only for comparison with the exact cut above, which it slightly under-cuts for fractions of a semitone. For changes of more than one semitone it over-cuts, compared with the exact cut without end correction.
